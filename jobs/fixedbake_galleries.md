- 2026-08-13 17:53 [overfit-test] Speckle-hypothesis test complete. Defined a spatial-coherence score for binary voxel masks: for every lit voxel, look at its six face neighbors that are also part of the object's own surface, take the fraction of those that are also lit, then average that fraction across all lit voxels that had at least one such neighbor. One means every lit voxel's neighbors agree with it; near zero means a lit voxel's neighbors almost never do. Measured it on all 23 gallery shapes (both their ground truth and their shown prediction), on a random sample of 100 training shapes' own labels, on the two examples the owner pointed to, and on the five draws already used in the threshold-sweep figure. Full numbers and the read are in a message to the team lead; short version: the correlation between how coherent a shape's ground truth is and how coherent its prediction is turns out weak on a straight measure and moderate on a rank-based one, and most of what looks like low coherence in the wider training sample is really just shapes with very few emissive voxels having little to agree with, not necessarily noisy labeling. The stronger, more direct finding is in the raw pre-threshold data: for both examples the owner flagged, more than half of the voxels counted as emissive have a raw brightness within one to three shades of the cutoff that decides emissive or not, which is a real, measured reason a fine texture near that boundary would flip labels almost at random.
